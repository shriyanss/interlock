# Source Audit — UA-2015 Blackout Mission

Companion to `/Users/shriyanss/GItHub/interlock/docs/research/ua-2015.md`. For each numbered source used there, this file states exactly which facts/claims were drawn from it, so a later reader can audit provenance per claim.

1. **CISA IR-ALERT-H-16-056-01** (https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/ics-alerts/ir-alert-h-16-056-01) — Basis for: the government-level description of the attack vector (spear-phishing with malicious Office attachments; BlackEnergy as delivery mechanism, with CISA explicitly noting the role of BlackEnergy itself in the outage "remains unknown"); the statement that attackers used existing OS-level remote administration tools and ICS client software over VPN, using previously acquired legitimate credentials; the statement that "malicious remote operation of the breakers was conducted by multiple external humans" (used to characterize the HMI-manipulation step as hands-on-keyboard); the KillDisk description (erasing files, corrupting MBR, overwriting Windows-based HMI/RTU systems); the serial-to-Ethernet device firmware corruption claim; the UPS scheduled-disconnect claim; and the ~225,000 customer-impact figure. Also the source for the note that CISA's page attributes the activity to "Russian nation-state cyber actors" as of its most recent update.

2. **MITRE ATT&CK Campaign C0028** (https://attack.mitre.org/campaigns/C0028/) — Basis for: the campaign's first-seen/last-seen window (December 2015–January 2016); attribution of the campaign to Sandworm Team (G0034); the malware pairing of BlackEnergy3 and KillDisk; the step-by-step attack-chain detail (VBA-macro-dropped BlackEnergy implant tracked as FONTCACHE.DAT, modified Dropbear SSH backdoor, creation of privileged domain accounts, network sniffing for credential capture, use of IT helpdesk software to reach ICS devices, VPN access using stolen domain credentials); the claim that breaker-opening operations across different companies occurred within roughly 30 minutes of each other; the serial-to-Ethernet converter firmware attack; the UPS shutdown scheduling; the TDoS against phone lines; and the approximate 6-hour blackout / manual-restoration outcome statement used in the Timeline and Attack Chain sections.

3. **MITRE ATT&CK Group G0034 (Sandworm Team)** (https://attack.mitre.org/groups/G0034/) — Basis for: the list of aliases attributed to Sandworm Team (ELECTRUM, Telebots, Iron Viking, BlackEnergy Group, Quedagh, Voodoo Bear, Iridium, Seashell Blizzard, FROZENBARENTS, APT44); the statement that the group is attributed to Russia's GRU Main Center for Special Technologies (GTsST), military unit 74455; the note on the October 2020 US indictment of six GRU Unit 74455 officers; and the framing, in the Attribution section, that MITRE's group page ties the 2015 Ukraine attack (Campaign C0028) to this same group/unit lineage.

4. **SANS Institute / E-ISAC, "Analysis of the Cyber Attack on the Ukrainian Power Grid: Defense Use Case"** (mirror: https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/sites/default/files/documents/3891751/SANS-and-Electricity-Information-Sharing-and.pdf; original https://ics.sans.org/media/E-ISAC_SANS_Ukraine_DUC_5.pdf, which redirected and could not be fetched directly) — Basis for: the March–May 2015 spear-phishing timeframe; the description of extended reconnaissance/dwell time before the December event; the detailed operator-HMI-manipulation and firmware-attack narrative; the KillDisk deployment against backup systems; the UPS disconnection and TDoS/call-center-disruption claims; the ~225,000-customer figure and "extended" outage-duration characterization; and the statement that remote breaker operation capability remained degraded for an extended period ("weeks") after the event, which is flagged in Open Questions as imprecise across secondary sources. This is treated as the most detailed technical/forensic source among those reviewed.

5. **CRS Report R48067, "Attacks on Ukraine's Electric Grid: Insights for U.S. Infrastructure Security and Resilience"** (https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48067; direct PDF fetch failed — returned a certificate/signature artifact rather than body text; accessed instead via the EveryCRSReport.com mirror, https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48067.html, and secondary search-result summaries) — Basis for: the Military/Geopolitical Context section's key data point that in July 2022, DTEK Group reported a Russian cyberattack on its IT infrastructure coinciding with a missile attack on one of its power stations — used specifically to illustrate a *documented* cyber-kinetic temporal correlation from the 2022 war, in contrast with the 2015 event, for which no such correlation is asserted anywhere in the sources reviewed. Also basis for general confirmation of US government attribution of grid-targeting activity to Russia. Flagged in Open Questions that full primary-text verification of this report was not achieved.

6. **Wikipedia, "2015 Ukraine power grid hack"** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Ukraine_power_grid_hack) — Used only as an index/cross-check, per task instructions, not cited as a standalone authority. Used to (a) resolve the exact three oblenergo names (Prykarpattyaoblenergo, Chernivtsioblenergo, Kyivoblenergo) against a search-engine-summarized version of the CISA page that had returned an inconsistent third name ("Odesaoblenergo") — Wikipedia's list matches the corroborating SANS/E-ISAC- and MITRE-derived reporting and was used to resolve that discrepancy in favor of Chernivtsioblenergo; (b) cross-check the substation count (30 substations, 7 at 110kV/23 at 35kV) and the ~73 MWh energy-not-served estimate, both flagged in ua-2015.md as reconstructed/approximate since they trace to secondary literature rather than being directly quoted in the primary documents; (c) confirm the general geopolitical framing (Donbas war, Crimea annexation) used in the Military/Geopolitical Context section.

7. **ESET / WeLiveSecurity, "BlackEnergy trojan strikes again: Attacks Ukrainian electric power industry"** (https://www.welivesecurity.com/2016/01/04/blackenergy-trojan-strikes-again-attacks-ukrainian-electric-power-industry/) — Basis for: the detailed KillDisk technical description (targeting of the `sec_service.exe` process, overwriting it with random data, time-delayed activation, association with ELTIMA Serial-to-Ethernet Connector or ASEM Ubiquity ICS software); the spear-phishing detail that some emails spoofed the Ukrainian parliament (Rada); the ~1.4 million population / roughly half of Ivano-Frankivsk region homes losing power for several hours; and the explicit statement, used in Open Questions, that ESET's article contains no mention of telephone DoS or UPS manipulation (i.e., those two claims are NOT independently corroborated by this ESET piece and rest on CISA/MITRE/SANS-E-ISAC instead).

8. **Secondary reporting on the TDoS/call-center disruption** (aggregated via web search; underlying claim traces to CISA [1] and SANS/E-ISAC [4]) — Basis for: the specific detail that Kyivoblenergo's call center was flooded with bogus automated calls from foreign numbers, preventing customers from reporting outages, used to add specificity beyond CISA/MITRE's more general "TDoS on phone lines" statement. Flagged as secondary/aggregated rather than a single primary document.

9. **U.S. Department of Justice, October 2020 indictment of six GRU Unit 74455 officers** (accessed via secondary reporting: Dark Reading, Security Magazine, Security Boulevard coverage of the unsealed indictment; original DOJ press release not directly fetched in this research pass) — Basis for: the Attribution section's claim that this indictment explicitly names "the 2015 and 2016 attacks against Ukrainian electrical companies" as part of the charged conduct, making it the most formal, unit-specific USG attribution statement identified for the December 2015 event. Flagged that the primary DOJ press release/indictment text itself was not directly retrieved; this rests on secondary journalistic summaries of it.

10. **Dragos, ELECTRUM group profile and 2015/10-year-retrospective blog content** (https://www.dragos.com/threat/electrum ; https://www.dragos.com/blog/2015-ukraine-power-grid-attack-lessons-in-defense) — Basis for: the Attribution section's discussion of ELECTRUM as a group Dragos associates with Sandworm activity around the 2015 timeframe, described as playing a development-support role at that time before taking on a combined development/operational role in the 2016 CRASHOVERRIDE/Industroyer incident; and the general corroboration of the ~225,000-customer, 30-substation, manual-restoration narrative. Accessed via search-result summaries rather than full-page fetch of every linked Dragos post; flagged as such.

## Note on unverifiable/AI-extraction artifact

One WebFetch-based extraction of the CISA alert page (source 1) returned a third oblenergo name of "Odesaoblenergo," which conflicts with every other source consulted (SANS/E-ISAC [4], MITRE [2], Wikipedia [6], and independent secondary search results), all of which name **Chernivtsioblenergo** as the third affected company. This is documented in `ua-2015.md`'s Affected Organizations and Open Questions sections as a probable extraction error rather than silently corrected or hidden, since the underlying CISA page itself was not read as raw text (only summarized by an intermediate model).
